Read the report at three levels

Start broad, then investigate. A low number is a prompt to look closer, not a complete explanation.

  • Class: which chapters or questions confused several learners?
  • Student: what has changed across this learner’s attempts?
  • Question: what did the student choose, and which step might explain the choice?
  • Use chapter and concept detail to connect the pattern with what was taught.

Turn a pattern into a teaching decision

Treat these as examples of how to investigate a report, rather than automatic diagnoses.

  • If many students miss the same concept, revisit the explanation with the class.
  • If a small group shares a gap, set a focused practice task or support session.
  • If answers are accurate but slow, inspect the method and time demands.
  • If a student repeatedly chooses the same distractor pattern, ask them to explain their reasoning.

Check the evidence behind a comparison

Context matters when you compare students, chapters, or tests.

  • Look at question counts before interpreting percentages.
  • Consider differences in difficulty and syllabus coverage between papers.
  • Read timing alongside accuracy rather than assuming faster always means better.
  • Check the student’s work before treating a wrong option as proof of a misconception.

Close the loop after the report

A useful follow-up is specific enough for a learner to act on and a teacher to revisit.

  • Name the concept or method to work on.
  • Choose a short revision activity or practice set.
  • Review another attempt to check whether the gap remains.

Questions, answered.

What can teachers see in VidyaRT reports?

Depending on the plan, reports include class results, student progress, chapter and concept detail, question responses, and timing information.

Are full reports included in Free?

Free includes a dashboard preview. Full class, student, and question reporting is available on paid plans.

Can a wrong answer prove a misconception?

A wrong answer is a useful clue, but not proof on its own. Review the question, the student’s working, and their explanation before deciding on follow-up.
